# Marc Rodriguez Personal Brand Site Claude Code Plan

Build a polished personal website for Marc Rodriguez that turns interest in his leadership, speaking, and entrepreneurial work into qualified inquiries. Marc should be the focus of the experience, with clear routes into Manifest Fitness Institute and Manifest Dallas.

The proposed launch priority is speaking inquiries, followed by professional connections and referrals to his businesses. This is a strategic recommendation for Marc to confirm. The brief below gives Claude Code enough direction to build a complete preview while keeping unapproved biography, offers, and media out of the public version.

Prepared October 30, 2026. Scope: implementation plan and handoff; no website has been built or deployed.

## Positioning

Proposed positioning: **A founder and speaker connecting leadership, entrepreneurship, and the business of fitness.**

Build the story around three connected themes: leading people, building businesses, and developing fitness professionals. His military experience gives the story context; his current work demonstrates how he applies it.

The visual identity should lead with “Marc Rodriguez.” Use “The Real Manifest CEO” as an optional social reference, subject to his preference. The two Manifest brands appear as ventures he leads, with their own links and identities.

# Visual direction

Recommended direction: **a polished editorial portrait site with warmth, authority, and real photography**.

The Institute’s current page loads Fraunces and Inter. Those families can provide a visual connection while the personal site gives Marc’s name, portrait, and voice greater prominence. This is a proposed design system, not a claim that the palette below is an official Manifest brand standard.

| Token | Proposed value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | #111111 | Hero, footer, primary text |
| Warm ivory | #F4F1EA | Main light sections |
| Bronze | #B89A63 | Small accents and buttons with dark text |
| Muted gray | #B8B6B0 | Supporting text on dark sections |
| Divider | #D8D2C7 | Rules on light backgrounds |

Verify contrast for every actual foreground/background combination. Bronze should not be used for small body text on ivory.

Typography: Fraunces for expressive display headings, Inter for navigation and body copy. Limit font weights and self-host production fonts when licensing permits. An existing repository’s established typography may take precedence if it fits the brief.

Layout: maximum content width around 1200–1280px; fluid gutters; 16–18px body text; restrained borders; consistent vertical rhythm. Alternate a dark hero with lighter reading sections. Use a text wordmark for launch; an optional MR monogram can follow.

Photography should feature Marc in three contexts: a strong portrait, speaking or teaching, and working inside Manifest. Prioritize real identity and clear faces. Preserve natural cropping on mobile. Use subtle overlays only where needed for legibility.

Motion: short, gentle opacity or position transitions; visible content without animation; respect reduced-motion settings. Keep normal scrolling and native cursor behavior. Avoid autoplay audio, video-dependent heroes, heavy parallax, and decorative effects that compete with the person.

# Functional and technical specification

## Repository and stack

First inspect the existing repository, README, AGENTS.md, package files, and design conventions. Preserve a suitable existing React stack. Do not replace working architecture merely to match this brief.

For a new project, the proposed default is Next.js App Router, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS, with mostly statically rendered content and a small server-side form endpoint. Use supported, compatible dependencies and current official documentation when implementing. Pin the resolved dependency versions in the lockfile.

Keep content in typed files for launch. Suggested organization:

- app: routes, page metadata, privacy page, contact endpoint, sitemap, and robots output.
- components: header, mobile navigation, hero, story preview, topic rows, venture panels, media section, contact form, footer.
- content: person, biography, topics, ventures, media, testimonials, and sources.
- lib: validation, email adapter, configuration, and analytics helpers.
- public: approved images, logos, video posters, and any actual downloadable files.
- docs: content gaps, asset register, launch checklist, and handoff notes.

Use content records with status values such as draft and approved, plus source URLs and approval notes. Public rendering includes approved entries only. Keep placeholder materials visibly labeled in a private preview and block production launch when essential content is unresolved.

No database, login, custom CMS, or customer dashboard is needed for the initial release. Add a CMS only if Marc needs direct editing.

## Contact form

Required fields: name, email, inquiry type, message. Optional fields: organization, event date, location or virtual, and website. Reveal event fields when Speaking is selected.

Inquiry types: Speaking, Media or Podcast, Partnership, General. Show separate links for education and training inquiries so visitors can reach the appropriate Manifest business.

Validate on both client and server. Include accessible errors, submitting state, success state, failure recovery, duplicate-submit prevention, a hidden spam field, and server-side abuse controls appropriate to the host. Preserve entered text after a recoverable error.

Send submissions through a configured server-side email provider or an existing approved form service. Keep credentials on the server. Use a verified sender and the visitor’s address as Reply-To. Escape user-entered content before composing HTML email.

The recipient must be supplied by Marc or Austin; do not assume the Institute’s general address is Marc’s personal booking inbox. Do not claim success when email delivery is unconfigured or the provider rejects the request. Confirm success only after the selected service accepts the submission; this does not prove inbox delivery.

In development, use an explicit test adapter or clearly labeled preview mode. Production must require a real provider configuration. An approved direct contact link can be a fallback.

Add a concise privacy notice. Contact inquiries do not automatically subscribe someone to a newsletter. Do not collect health histories or payment information.

## Media, external links, and analytics

Use approved local images in modern formats, responsive sizes, explicit dimensions, and appropriate alternative text. Prioritize the hero image and lazy-load content below the fold. Use click-to-load video embeds with descriptive fallback links.

External business links may include a consistent UTM campaign for attribution, configured centrally after the personal domain is chosen. Preserve destination query parameters. Clearly identify business destinations.

Track speaking CTA clicks, business referrals, and accepted form submissions. Do not send form content, names, or email addresses to analytics. Keep analytics optional until a provider and privacy settings are selected.

## Search and social sharing

Give every route a descriptive title, unique meta description, canonical URL, and social preview image. Suggested home title: “Marc Rodriguez | Speaker and Manifest Founder.”

Use Person structured data with confirmed identity, roles, and supplied social links. Represent the Manifest organizations separately where needed; do not describe Marc’s personal site as the gym’s local-business listing.

Generate a sitemap and robots rules. Keep preview environments out of search results; remove preview noindex settings at launch. Configure the final domain centrally. Domain selection and availability remain open.

# Implementation sequence and acceptance criteria

## Phase 1 Foundation

Inspect the repository and establish the design tokens, route structure, typed content, asset register, and configuration. Create docs/CONTENT_GAPS.md with each missing item, its owner, and whether it blocks production. Record the source for factual copy.

## Phase 2 Visual build

Build the responsive header, footer, and complete homepage first. Continue through About, Speaking, Contact, Privacy, and 404 using shared components. Keep the initial preview useful with approved facts and clearly identified draft content.

## Phase 3 Integrations

Implement the contact workflow and its error states. Add media only when supplied, business referral tracking, metadata, structured data, sitemap, and optional analytics. Keep external service details configurable.

## Phase 4 Verification and handoff

Run the repository’s relevant type, lint, and production-build checks. Verify the main visitor journeys and inspect desktop and mobile layouts. Fix actual failures before extending scope.

Acceptance criteria:

- The first screen identifies Marc, his focus, and the primary next step.
- All navigation, business links, and speaking CTA preselection work.
- Layouts remain readable at 360, 390, 768, and 1440px with no horizontal overflow.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, mobile menu dismissal, form labels, and errors work.
- Heading order is logical; images have appropriate alternative text; reduced-motion preferences are respected.
- Normal text meets a 4.5:1 contrast target; large text meets 3:1.
- Form validation, accepted submission, provider failure, and spam handling are tested meaningfully.
- No missing credentials can produce a misleading submission success message.
- A controlled end-to-end inquiry reaches the configured recipient before launch.
- No invented achievements, unapproved quotes, broken downloads, placeholder images, or draft topic claims appear in the public build.
- Production metadata uses the correct domain; preview environments remain excluded from indexing.
- Audit mobile performance after real media is loaded. Target LCP at or below 2.5 seconds and CLS at or below 0.1; measure responsiveness where supported, and distinguish lab findings from field data.
- Provide setup instructions, environment-variable names, a content-editing guide, known limitations, and the completed launch checklist.

Do not deploy automatically from this planning brief. Finish with a working preview and a concrete list of content or configuration that still blocks public launch. Domain registration and paid service purchases require separate direction.
